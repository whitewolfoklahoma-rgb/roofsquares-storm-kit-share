The money — deductible & depreciation, in plain numbers

Most replacement-cost policies pay in two parts: an actual-cash-value (ACV) payment up front, and the recoverable depreciation held back until the work is done and documented. This tracker keeps the holdback visible so it doesn't get forgotten — forgotten depreciation is money the homeowner (and the job) never sees.

How these numbers work: depreciation held back = RCV − deductible − ACV received. Still recoverable = held back − already released. Total the carrier pays on a completed, documented job = RCV − deductible. These are planning figures from the numbers you enter — the carrier's own estimate and policy control the actual payments.

Help — how this kit works

The workflow

1. After a storm, open a Job File and complete the Inspection on site — severity, findings, notes, and photo references while you're on the roof.
2. In the Claim Packet, add the carrier and claim details, tick off each document as you send it, and print or adapt the claim summary letter.
3. Leave the Homeowner Pack behind: the door hanger for the street, the what-to-expect sheet for the customer, and a signed tarping agreement before any temporary repair.
4. Work the Claim Tracker until the job is closed — stage by stage, with the depreciation holdback in plain sight the whole time. Pair it with your RoofSquares estimate for the repair/replacement numbers themselves.

Photo references, not photos

Like the rest of this product line, the kit stores photo file names and descriptions, not image files. Keep the originals in your camera roll or cloud drive, backed up — the log tells you (and the carrier) exactly which photo shows what.

Depreciation, plainly

On a replacement-cost (RCV) policy the carrier usually pays ACV (today's depreciated value) first, minus the deductible, and holds the depreciation until the work is complete and documented (final invoice + photos). If nobody asks for the release, it typically expires unclaimed — commonly within a policy deadline. This kit's tracker exists so that never happens on your jobs. Deadlines and rules are set by the homeowner's own policy — check theirs.

Disclaimers — read these

This kit is a documentation and organization aid. It is not legal advice, not insurance advice, and not a public-adjusting service. The letter and agreement templates are general business documents — have a local attorney review anything you rely on, and never sign documents on a homeowner's behalf or represent yourself as an adjuster (in many states that's licensed work, and in some states contractors are restricted in how they may negotiate claims — know your state's rules). The kit makes no promise about claim outcomes: it does not use Xactimate, does not estimate what a carrier will pay, and no template here can make a carrier approve anything. The money figures are arithmetic on the numbers you type in.
